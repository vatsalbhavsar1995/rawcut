# 1️⃣ THE FULL STORY — Chapter by Chapter (Studio Pitch Format)

## LOGLINE
> A deep-cover RAW operative embedded for 12 years in a hostile port city has 5 days to stop a detonation he unknowingly helped fund — while the traitor giving him orders sits inside his own agency.

## THEMES
Identity vs. duty · patience as a weapon · the cover becomes the trap · **the operative wins** — not tragically, not ambiguously. Cleanly. This is the anti-typical spy ending.

## TONE REFERENCES
*URI* (tactical authenticity) · *Dhurandhar* (deep-cover grit, enemy-territory texture) · *Special Ops* (long-con architecture) · *Tiger* (operatic scale) — executed with SUPERHOT's frozen-time discipline and Uncharted: Lost Legacy's chapter craft.

## DAY 1 — "THE WRONG MAN" *(HOURS REMAINING: 120)*

**Chapter 1.1 — "The Impound"**
Night. Police impound yard. The syndicate's seized shipment must be recovered before it's catalogued. Tutorial-as-drama: frozen-time infiltration through floodlit corridors of sleeping officers, evidence shelves, caged motor pool. Kaif plants an internal-affairs dossier that frames a corrupt inspector — the syndicate never learns a spy did this.

*Chapter ends: the shipment manifest has a line item that shouldn't exist — "SRV-C. Maintenance contract, downtown."*

**Chapter 1.2 — "The Flower Shop"**
Daytime bazaar. Kaif's RAW dead-drop contact operates from a flower stall. Following a chalk mark, receiving a hollowed Quran-broker's ledger — played entirely as Cover Meter gameplay: shop, greet, barter, and never once look like an operative in a crowd of 40 ambient NPCs.

*Night intel scene (safehouse — his shipping-container office of 12 years): handler's voice ("DAKSH"):* **"The material never left the country. It's here. Five days. A server center downtown is the trigger. Find the courier."**

**Chapter 1.3 — "Night Watch"**
The port at night — the same map as 1.2's bazaar, transformed: rain, shuttered stalls, searchlights. First kill-room: syndicate muscle sweeping the flower stall's owner. Frozen-time rescue in the alley — first blood, first consequence.

*End of Day 1 reveal:* the flower seller, dying: *"The courier doesn't carry anything. He carries a *schedule*."*

## DAY 2 — "THE COURIER" *(96 HOURS)*

**Chapter 2.1 — "The Tail"**
Daylight. Pure surveillance gameplay — follow the courier through the bazaar *as Kaif Alvi, businessman*, greeting people who know him, buying things, while reading the courier's route. Get spotted twice and the courier changes pattern permanently (state change, not fail state).

**Chapter 2.2 — "The Dead End"**
The courier walks into a trap that isn't Kaif's — a second hunter team takes him. Frozen-time combat: Kaif must take the courier *from* the rival hunters without revealing himself to either side. The set-piece: a spice warehouse where every frozen cloud of turmeric hangs like smoke in stopped time — the game's first poster shot.

*Night intel:* the courier's schedule chip decodes. The rival hunters' comms intercept — and DAKSH goes silent for three full seconds, then: *"Impossible."* **The rival team is RAW's vanished Unit-9. Believed KIA two years ago. Not dead. Hiding. From someone inside.**

## DAY 3 — "THE LEDGER" *(72 HOURS)*

**Chapter 3.1 — "The Daughter"**
The syndicate boss's daughter — **Zoya** — summons Kaif. She's found something in the books: the server center's maintenance contract traces back through three shells to *Alvi Shipping*. Kaif's own cover company funded the trigger. She doesn't know what it means. He does. She gives him the ledger as leverage — *"Explain this to me, or I show my father."*

**Chapter 3.2 — "Warehouse Betrayal"**
The syndicate's internal security chief has spotted the anomaly independently — mid-meeting, the warehouse turns: frozen-time massacre set-piece, Kaif escaping wounded through stacked containers while preserving cover with the survivors ("it was Unit-9, I saw them"). Biggest combat chapter of the game.

**Chapter 3.3 — "The Old Friend"**
Wounded, Kaif reaches Unit-9's safehouse. Faces **Rehman** — the colleague he saved years ago, the man who "died" so the op could stay deniable. Rehman works maintenance *inside the server center* — placed there to watch the trigger. He confirms: the countdown is real. And adds the gut punch:

*"Your handler. DAKSH. Unit-9 didn't go dark, Kaif. We were burned. By the same channel that talks to you every night."*

*End of Day 3: the enemy is inside RAW.*

## DAY 4 — "THE INSIDE MAN" *(48 HOURS)*

**Chapter 4.1 — "The Extraction"**
Rehman must be pulled before the syndicate sweeps him — one sprawling infiltration through the server center's service levels: three authored approaches (ventilation/combat, social-engineering as Alvi the IT contractor, night stealth), converging on one extraction. The Lost Legacy showpiece level — the map players will revisit on Day 5, *changed*.

**Chapter 4.2 — "The Split"**
Zoya finds Kaif at the safehouse. She's decoded the ledger fully — and brought the boss's private schedule: her father is being *set up as the bomber*. A fall guy. The real hand is elsewhere. She wants in. Kaif sends her out with the evidence and a burner phone — *"If I don't call by tomorrow night, take it to the consulate. Not RAW. The consulate."*

*Night intel — the twist detonates:* DAKSH's orders for Day 5 arrive. They're perfect. Too perfect. Cross-checking with Rehman's access logs: **DAKSH's channel is the mole's channel. Every extraction order, every safehouse, every dead-drop — Kaif has been led by the conspiracy for months. His extraction tomorrow is his assassination.**

## DAY 5 — "BLAST OFF" *(24 HOURS — no safehouse, no handler, no HUD handler voice)*

**Chapter 5.1 — "Ghost Protocol"**
Kaif goes dark — enters the server center through the route only *he* knows (his own company serviced the building for 12 years; every blueprint beat planted since Day 1 pays off here). The handler voice is gone — replaced by Zoya on the burner and Rehman on the internal cameras. The player feels the absence of the voice that's talked to them for 12 hours of gameplay. That silence IS the atmosphere.

**Chapter 5.2 — "The Mirror"**
Deep in the server core: the mole's inside man — **"SAARANG"** — an off-book RAW rival operative with the *same frozen-time discipline*. The boss fight: an equal. A duel of patience where every move you make, he makes one too — the mechanic weaponized against the player for the first time. Defeating him means forcing him to *move first*.

**Chapter 5.3 — "Blast Off"**
The finale: Kaif doesn't cut the detonation signal — he **reroutes it through DAKSH's own channel**, forcing the entire mole network to expose itself in one transmission burst. The server center blows — and the explosion *is the victory*: the conspiracy's whole architecture burns in the flash. Rehman walks out. The material is secured. The traitors are named.

**FINAL CINEMATIC — "The Pier"** *(the exact pier from Chapter 1.1's impound approach, now at dawn)*
Kaif walks the harbor as the call to prayer sounds. He removes the Alvi watch — the Cover Meter UI itself — and drops it into the sea. Burner rings. Zoya: *"So what do we call you now?"*

He answers with his real name. First time in the entire game the audience hears it.

**Cut to black. Stamp SFX. One case file slams shut: CLOSED.**

5. **Art pass only on rooms that already play well.** This discipline is why "solo dev in months" is achievable

# 6️⃣ Godot or Unreal — Terminal-Driven, You Brushing

**Both work from terminal. Pick one, don't revisit.**

## Godot 4 (my recommendation for this project)
- **Fully headless/CLI-capable:** run scenes, import assets, export builds, run unit tests — all scriptable; Claude Code edits GDScript/.tscn directly and you hot-reload
- Text-based scene files = AI agents can read and modify *entire levels* as code. Unreal's binary maps are agent-hostile; Godot's are plain text — **this matters more than any rendering difference for an agentic workflow**
- Lighter, faster iteration loop, SDFGI/volumetric fog gives you the cinematic lighting our art bible demands
- Solo-dev scale: this is the honest engine for a 13-chapter, 4-environment game

## Unreal 5
- If you want Lumen/Nanite out of the box and plan a AAA-look pitch, it's viable — but binary assets resist AI iteration, and the editor workflow fights the terminal pipeline
- Heavier, slower, more crash-prone for one person

**Decision: Godot 4. Terminal-native, agent-friendly, and good enough lighting that low-poly looks like a poster.**

# 7️⃣ Character Development — The Hard Problem, Solved

Characters are where indies fail visibly. Three-tier solution:

## Tier 1 — Source base meshes (week 1)
- Quaternius/Kenney low-poly humans as blockers; Mixamo rigging
- Kaif: one model, three costume states (businessman Alvi / operative gear / wounded Day 3–4 state — costume change as narrative progress, visible in every cutscene)

## Tier 2 — Identity through silhouette + palette, not detail
- **Kaif:** slim silhouette, the watch (UI + character in one object), a single accent color (deep green scarf — appears on Zoya's gift in the finale)
- **Zoya:** sharp angular silhouette, gold accents — she reads instantly in a crowd
- **Rehman:** broad, weathered, grey — the brother-in-arms read
- **SAARANG (boss):** Kaif's silhouette *inverted palette* — mirror made visual
- Low-poly faces don't need sculpting; they need **distinctive shapes + one strong color each.** That's a designer decision, not an art-skill problem — and agents can generate the variants for you to select.

## Tier 3 — Character as performance (the COD/MW trick)
You referenced MW's unskippable cinematics — the secret is **camera + audio, not polygon count.** Our characters get their "motion-capture" from:
- Frozen-time tableau framing (characters posed like film stills — the mechanic *is* the cutscene direction)
- Letterboxed framing, slow push-ins, film grain — cheap techniques that read as prestige
- VO carrying emotion (ElevenLabs-quality synthesis + your direction), faces doing minimal stylized animation

This is how a low-poly game earns MW-level cinematic weight: **the audience's imagination fills what the polygons leave out — if your direction gives them a frame worth imagining.**
